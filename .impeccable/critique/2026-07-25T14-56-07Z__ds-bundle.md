## Design Specificity Verdict

**A real token layer sitting on a generic component skeleton — thin but genuine identity, undermined by consistency gaps.**

**LLM assessment**: The kit has actual signs of a point of view: domain-specific semantic colors (`critical`/`official`/`dispute` instead of literal red/green/amber, mapped to race-timing dispute workflows), a saturated `blue-700` instead of the ubiquitous `indigo-600`, `JetBrains Mono` reserved specifically for tabular race numbers, and a hard-cornered, shadow-less surface language applied with real discipline across all 12 components. That's more specificity than most generated design systems show.

But the component *logic* underneath is close to a Tailwind tutorial: `Button`'s variants are textbook `bg-blue-700 hover:bg-blue-800` / `border-zinc-200 hover:bg-zinc-50`, the type scale never goes beyond `text-xs`/`sm`/`lg`/`xl` with no distinctive heading treatment (there's no Heading component at all across 12 components), and there isn't a single icon anywhere in the kit — not even for error/empty/loading states, which conventionally lean on iconography. `Inter`/`system-ui` as the sans stack is the single most common "this was AI-generated" tell.

**Deterministic scan**: `detect.mjs` returned 16 findings (exit 2): `single-font` (only system-ui rendered), `side-tab` and `border-accent-on-rounded` (inconsistent border-accent conventions — `border-l-2` and `border-b-2` used for what should be one consistent selection idiom), 11× `gray-on-color` (gray/zinc text over `bg-blue-50`), and `monotonous-spacing` (~8px spacing used in 12 of 14 measured gaps, 86%). Assessment B flagged that the 11 `gray-on-color` hits are a static CSS co-presence check, not a confirmed rendered-DOM pairing — treat those as directional, not proven, contrast failures. No other false positives found; the rest are real and match what the LLM review independently found by computing actual contrast ratios from the token hex values (see Priority Issues below).

**Visual overlays**: Browser script injection succeeded and surfaced 2 additional live-DOM findings not visible from static file scanning: `line-length` (~187 chars/line on a `<p>`, well past the 80-char readability target) and `flat-type-hierarchy` (body text sizes cluster at 13/16/20px, a 1.5:1 ratio — a flat, low-contrast type scale, corroborating the LLM's "no distinctive heading treatment" finding independently). No mutable browser tab was left open for you to inspect — the reviewing agent's browser session and injected script were torn down as part of cleanup; re-run `/impeccable audit ds-bundle` if you want a fresh live overlay.

## Overall Impression

This is a design system with one strong idea (domain-real semantic color names, disciplined flat/square surface language) wearing generic-tutorial clothing everywhere else — and it has real, mechanically-confirmed bugs, not just taste gaps. The most damaging finding isn't aesthetic: **11 of 12 components ship with zero accessibility attributes**, focus rings vanish on the two most-clicked elements (Button, Tabs), and the `.review.html` contact sheet itself is silently rendering a broken Modal (collapsed to `maxHeight: 0`) alongside real React console warnings (missing `key` props across 9+ components). The single biggest opportunity: propagate the one genuine identity signal you have (the `critical`/`official`/`dispute` vocabulary) system-wide, and do one dedicated accessibility pass before this becomes the foundation other screens are built on.

## What's Working

- **Domain-specific semantic color naming** — `critical`/`official`/`dispute` tokens tied to real race-timing dispute states, not generic red/green/amber. This is the strongest and most defensible design decision in the whole kit.
- **Modal's focus management** (`_ds_bundle.js:132-168`) — captures the previously-focused element, autofocuses the first focusable descendant, restores focus on unmount, closes on Escape and true backdrop click (correctly ignores bubbled clicks). Genuinely above-average for generated output, and it's the only component with any ARIA (`role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`, `aria-labelledby`).
- **Disciplined, consistent restraint** — no gratuitous shadows or rounded corners, flat bordered surfaces applied uniformly across every one of the 12 components. Whatever the visual stance is, it's applied with a steady hand.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Button and Tabs have no focus-visible ring, while Input/Select/Textarea do**
Why it matters: keyboard users get a visible ring on form fields but silently lose focus indication on every button and every tab — confirmed independently by both the code review (`_ds_bundle.js:112-127`, `:243-257` have no `focus:`/`focus-visible:` class) and the mechanical a11y grep (Assessment B: only Modal has any explicit a11y/focus attribute; 11 of 12 components have none). This is a WCAG 2.4.7 failure, not a style nitpick.
Fix: add a shared `focus-visible:ring-2 focus-visible:ring-offset-1 focus-visible:ring-blue-700` utility to Button's variant/size classes and to the tab-button className in Tabs.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

**[P0] ErrorAlert has no `role="alert"`, and the whole a11y surface is nearly empty across the kit**
Why it matters: ErrorAlert's Spanish copy is genuinely specific and actionable ("El dorsal ingresado no existe en esta carrera"), but with no `role="alert"`/`aria-live`, a screen-reader user who triggers an error hears nothing. This isn't isolated — the mechanical grep confirms 11 of 12 components ship zero `aria-`/`role=`/`tabIndex` attributes (Tabs has no `role="tablist"/"tab"`/`aria-selected`; DataTable's `<th>` has no `scope`; LoadingText has no `role="status"`). Good copy, no delivery mechanism.
Fix: add `role="alert"` to ErrorAlert, `role="status"`/`aria-live="polite"` to LoadingText, proper tab/tablist ARIA to Tabs, and `scope="col"` to DataTable headers.
Suggested command: `/impeccable audit`

**[P1] Confirmed contrast failures on `official` and `dispute` tinted text, at the sizes actually used**
Why it matters: computed WCAG contrast ratios from the real token hex values — `official-600` (#059669) on `official-50` (#ecfdf5) = 3.58:1, `dispute-600` (#d97706) on `dispute-50` (#fffbeb) = 3.07:1, both used at `text-xs` (12px) on `MetricCard`'s status labels. Both fail AA's 4.5:1 minimum for normal text. This is exactly the "at-a-glance dashboard number" surface where legibility matters most, and it's currently the least legible text in the kit. The detector's 11 `gray-on-color` hits are a weaker, unconfirmed version of the same signal — this LLM-computed ratio is the harder evidence.
Fix: darken `-600` tokens toward `-700` for on-tint text, or move label text off the tinted background onto zinc-900/white.
Suggested command: `/impeccable colorize`

**[P1] MetricCard's `orange` and `amber` variants are dead — they render pixel-identical styling**
Why it matters: this was independently confirmed by the mechanical grep (`_ds_bundle.js:224-230`, `VARIANT_CLASSES2` maps both variant names to the identical `dispute-*` classes) — two variant names exist in the API surface but only one visual outcome exists, which is either a copy-paste bug or an abandoned distinction. Either way, a consumer picking `orange` vs `amber` gets no signal they made a meaningful choice, and it's exactly the kind of one-deep inconsistency that erodes trust in a design system's contract.
Fix: either merge the two variant names into one, or give `orange` and `amber` genuinely distinct token mappings.
Suggested command: `/impeccable audit`

**[P2] The Modal on the review page renders collapsed (`maxHeight: 0`), and 9+ components throw React "missing key" console warnings**
Why it matters: this is not a taste issue, it's a functional regression the mechanical render-check already flags (`.render-check.json`: Modal is the only one of 12 entries with `thin: true, collapsed: true, maxHeight: 0`, visually confirmed as "Modal ⚠ thin" on the contact sheet) plus real React warnings ("Each child in a list should have a unique 'key' prop") firing repeatedly across LoadingText, ErrorAlert, DataTable, Select, and others. A design system with a broken flagship component in its own showcase page undermines confidence in everything else in this critique.
Fix: investigate why Modal collapses in the review harness (likely a missing height/flex constraint on its wrapper) and add stable `key` props everywhere a list is mapped.
Suggested command: `/impeccable audit`

## Persona Red Flags

**Sam (Accessibility-dependent)**: Tabbing through any screen built from this kit, Sam loses visible focus position on every button and every tab (P0) — only form fields keep the ring. Submitting a form and hitting a save error, Sam's screen reader announces nothing (ErrorAlert has no `role="alert"`). Sam's screen reader also announces `Tabs` as a flat list of unrelated buttons, not a tab interface, since there's no `role="tablist"`/`aria-selected` — and Sam can't arrow-key between tabs, only Tab+Enter one at a time. At `MetricCard`'s intended 12px label size, Sam with low vision can't reliably read the "official"/"dispute" status text (confirmed sub-AA contrast).

**Alex (Power user)**: Triaging hundreds of race results in `DataTable`, Alex has no column sort, no filter, no bulk-select, and no keyboard row navigation — every action is mouse-driven, one row at a time. Switching between sections built on `Tabs`, Alex gets no keyboard shortcuts (no arrow-key roving, no `Ctrl+1..N`), just repeated clicking. Trying to compose `DataTable` + `ErrorAlert` + `LoadingText` into one async table, Alex has no documented pattern to follow — all 12 `.prompt.md` files just restate the `.d.ts` prop shapes, so Alex has to reverse-engineer the intended composition from scratch.

**Riley (Stress tester)**: Riley opens the kit's own `.review.html` contact sheet and immediately finds a broken component in the showcase itself — Modal renders collapsed to zero height (mechanically confirmed via `.render-check.json`), and the console is full of repeated React key warnings across half the kit. Riley also finds that `MetricCard`'s `orange` and `amber` variants are supposed to be different but render identically — the kind of silent, half-finished distinction that stress-testing exists to catch.

## Minor Observations

- `_ds_bundle.css` defines `--radius-lg` and `.shadow-sm`/`.shadow-md` utilities that no component ever consumes — dead tokens worth pruning, or a sign a "raised" treatment (e.g. for Modal) was planned and dropped.
- `Modal`'s `maxWidth` prop only accepts `"md" | "lg"` — a narrow range for a general-purpose modal.
- `LoadingText`'s default message ("Cargando...") is hardcoded Spanish with no i18n hook — fine for a single-locale tool today, worth flagging if NicaRunner ever needs bilingual UI.
- `Button` defaults `type="button"` explicitly — a good defensive default that prevents accidental form submits; worth calling out as something done right.
- The bundle actually ships **12 components**, not the 11 implied by the two top-level category folders (form/ has 4, general/ has 8) — worth double-checking your own inventory docs if you're tracking coverage elsewhere.

## Questions to Consider

- If Button and Tabs are missing focus rings while Input/Select/Textarea have them, was there ever a single accessibility pass across the whole kit — or was each component built independently against its own prompt with no cross-component consistency check?
- The `critical`/`official`/`dispute` vocabulary is the strongest identity signal in the kit and it lives inside exactly one component (`MetricCard`) plus `ErrorAlert`'s copy. Is there a plan to propagate it into a Badge/Tag/Status primitive, or does the system's "point of view" stay one component deep?
- Given the Modal is both the most accessibility-mature component (the only one with ARIA) *and* the one that's currently broken in its own showcase — is there a regression test running anywhere, or did this ship without anyone opening `.review.html` after the last change?
